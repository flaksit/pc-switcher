### What has no VM coverage at all

- The apt origin model: a real install landing from a derived vendor repository, and the post-refresh verification refusing a wrong-origin install. Candidate selection, epochs and pin priorities are the point, and mocks cannot settle them.
- Apt hold replication in every form: a hold that travels, a held package installed at Atlas's exact version, a bookkeeping hold cleared and re-registered. The design rests on measured `apt-mark`/`apt-get` behaviour, and mocks assert only that the commands we think we send are sent.
- Collateral damage as a whole — no integration test asserts anything about it. The two accepted gaps turn on what real apt does that the rehearsal does not: real cascade behaviour and real `--allow-change-held-packages` semantics.
- snapd's pre-removal snapshot surviving a removal through a sync.
- The auto-refresh suspension being a timed value that lapses on its own, including after a run that dies mid-window.
- A deleted flatpak remote taking its signing key with it, which is delegated to flatpak.
- A real bare `.deb` on a machine, where apt reports the installed version as its own candidate, and what the unreproducible scan names on a stock machine.
- The `folder_sync` boundary: no integration run enables a package job and `folder_sync` together, so neither the `~/snap` revision boundary nor the snippet registry bypass is observed on real machines.
- That a whole sync leaves Atlas's own installed software and origins unchanged.
